## 17. A point-in-time defect in the stored macro series (`8400321`)

Wiring the macro consumer turned one up. `CPIAUCSL.csv` holds observation `2010-01-01` with
`availableAt 2010-02-16` and value `217.488`. The vintage history shows **217.488 is the
2015-02-28 revision**; what was printed in February 2010 was `217.587`.

**The CSV's `value` column is the latest revision while its `availableAt` is the first
publication date.** Anything joining on `availableAt` and reading `value` gets a figure up to
five years early. **10 of the 32 series are revision-tracked and carry this hazard.**

`tools/foundation/macro_context.py` contains it: where a vintage exists it is the *only* source
of the value, and the reading is the last revision published on or before the decision date. The
22 unrevised series keep their own `availableAt`, which is authoritative for them. Nothing found
returns `None` — never a default — and `notKnowable` names what could not be read.

14 tests run against the real stored files, including a sweep asserting that across four
decision dates **no series ever returns a value whose `availableAt` is later than the date**, and
one asserting the record carries no field named direction, vote, signal, bias, action or score.
